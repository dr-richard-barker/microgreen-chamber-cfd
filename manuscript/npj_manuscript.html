Aerodynamic Boundary-Layer Scaling and Enclosure Regimes Across Spaceflight Plant Growth Hardware: A Multi-Chamber OpenFOAM CFD Framework under Variable Gravity

Richard Barker1,*, Henry Ewald1, and Astrobotany Consortium1
1 Department of Agricultural and Biological Engineering, Purdue University, West Lafayette, IN 47907, USA
* Corresponding author: rbarker@purdue.edu

Abstract

Plants cultivated in extraterrestrial habitats encounter a physical environment devoid of natural gravity-driven buoyancy (\(Gr o 0\)), expanding unstirred fluid boundary layers around vegetative canopies and drastically elevating aerodynamic resistance (\(r_a = 1/g_{bl}\)). Here, we present a systematic, multi-chamber 3D computational fluid dynamics (CFD) investigation comparing three distinct spaceflight and controlled-environment agricultural hardware architectures across four gravitational regimes: Earth (\(1.0 ext{ g}\)), Mars (\(0.38 ext{ g}\)), Moon (\(0.166 ext{ g}\)), and Microgravity (\(0 ext{ g}\)). Using an OpenFOAM v2606 finite-volume framework with conformal multi-solid analytic geometries, we model: (i) the compact Microgreen Chamber (\(2.33 ext{ L}\), through-flow confined jet), (ii) the NASA Vegetable Production System (VEGGIE/VPS) (\(37.6 ext{ L}\), top suction with passive cabin air induction), and (iii) the NASA Advanced Plant Habitat (APH) (\(83.4 ext{ L}\), ducted closed-loop opposing cross-flow). Parametric gravity sweeps reveal that ceiling-mounted LED arrays induce stable thermal stratification on Earth (\(Ri pprox 0.14 - 1.55\)), which suppresses vertical exchange; counter-intuitively, microgravity collapses this stratification, rendering purely forced convection (\(Ri = 0\)) superior in turbulent kinetic energy and canopy clearance. In VEGGIE, low-fan microgravity operation leads to a critical \(52.8\%\) canopy stagnation volume and a \(39.5\%\) reduction in \(CO_2\) boundary layer conductance (\(g_{bl} = 0.219 ext{ mol m}^{-2} ext{s}^{-1}\)), elevating fungal mold vulnerability and guttation stress. Conversely, APH's opposing lateral supply jets (\(0.3 - 1.5 ext{ m/s}\)) maintain near-ideal displacement ventilation (air exchange efficiency \( arepsilon_a pprox 45\%\)) and invariant \(g_{bl} pprox 1.07 ext{ mol m}^{-2} ext{s}^{-1}\) across all gravity levels. Analysis of suspended bioaerosols establishes a fundamental biosecurity trade-off: VEGGIE exports \(100\%\) of aerosolized fungal spores directly into astronaut living quarters (\(t_{50} = 13.8 ext{ s}\)), whereas APH achieves rapid internal HEPA scrubbing (\(t_{50} = 18.4 ext{ s}\)) with zero cabin burden. These findings establish quantitative aerodynamic criteria for designing next-generation bioregenerative life support systems for Lunar and Martian surface missions.

Introduction

Sustained human exploration of the Moon and Mars under NASA's Artemis and Moon-to-Mars architectures necessitates robust Bioregenerative Life Support Systems (BLSS) capable of producing fresh nutritious crops, recycling water, and revitalizing habitat atmospheres. In spaceflight environments, the absence of natural gravitational acceleration fundamentally alters fluid physics: natural thermal convection ceases (\(Gr o 0, Ra o 0\)), removing the buoyancy forces that drive passive air circulation and scalar transport around terrestrial plant canopies.

Under microgravity (\(\mu ext{g}\)), leaves become encased in thickened, stagnant fluid boundary layers, severely increasing aerodynamic resistance (\(r_a = 1/g_{bl}\)) to carbon dioxide (\(CO_2\)) assimilation, transpirational cooling, and volatile release. Without adequate forced convective stirring, localized \(CO_2\) depletion within the canopy suppresses RuBisCO carboxylation efficiency and triggers photorespiratory stress, while transpirational moisture accumulation creates persistent boundary-layer humidity (\(RH > 90\%\)), leading to stomatal dysregulation, hyper-guttation, and devastating fungal phytopathogen outbreaks, such as the Fusarium oxysporum epidemic observed during early VEG-01 flight validation.

Gravity Scaling and the Richardson Number Regime Transition

To quantify the transition between buoyancy-driven and momentum-driven convection, we conducted parametric gravity sweeps across \(g \in \{9.81, 3.72, 1.62, 0.00\} ext{ m/s}^2\), evaluating the dimensionless Grashof (\(Gr\)), Reynolds (\(Re\)), and Richardson (\(Ri\)) numbers:

\[ Gr = rac{g eta \Delta T L^3}{ u^2}, \quad Re = rac{U L}{ u}, \quad Ri = rac{Gr}{Re^2} = rac{g eta \Delta T L}{U^2} \]

As illustrated in Figure 2 and Table 2, VEGGIE under Low Fan at \(1 ext{ g}\) operates at \(Ri = 1.551\), placing it in the buoyancy-dominated mixed convection regime. When gravity is removed in microgravity (\(0 ext{ g}\)), natural thermal buoyancy vanishes completely (\(Gr = 0, Ri = 0\)). Without the buoyancy-assisted chimney effect, low-fan operation fails to penetrate the dense plant canopy, resulting in a critical \(52.8\%\) stagnant volume fraction. Conversely, APH maintains \(Ri \le 0.1246\) at \(1 ext{ g}\) and \(Ri \le 0.0473\) under planetary gravities, remaining strictly within the forced convective regime across all missions.

Canopy Boundary-Layer Conductance and Microclimatic Stress

Boundary-layer conductance to \(CO_2\) mass transfer (\(g_{bl}\)) dictates photosynthetic carbon assimilation and transpirational cooling:

\[ Sh_{CO2} = rac{h_m d_{leaf}}{D_{CO2}}, \quad g_{bl} = rac{D_{CO2} Sh_{CO2}}{d_{leaf}} \cdot \left( rac{P}{R T} ight) \]

As detailed in Table 3 and Figure 3, APH maintains an exceptionally high and uniform boundary layer conductance (\(g_{bl} = 1.071 - 1.102 ext{ mol m}^{-2} ext{s}^{-1}\)) under nominal \(0.6 ext{ m/s}\) supply across all gravities. In contrast, VEGGIE under Low Fan in microgravity suffers a severe conductance reduction down to \(0.219 ext{ mol m}^{-2} ext{s}^{-1}\) (\(\delta_{bl} = 7.95 ext{ mm}\)), creating a rate-limiting aerodynamic resistance (\(r_a = 4.56 ext{ m}^2 ext{s/mol}\)) that exceeds stomatal resistance, explaining the localized high humidity and tipburn observed during ISS growth cycles.

Scalar Residence Time, Age of Air, and Bioaerosol Biosecurity

Local Mean Age of Air (\( au\)) and Air Exchange Efficiency (\( arepsilon_a = au_0 / (2 \overline{ au}_{canopy}) imes 100\%\)) reveal that APH achieves near-ideal displacement ventilation (\( arepsilon_a pprox 45\%\)) with negligible trapped volume (\(<8.5\%\)), holding local canopy \(CO_2\) drawdown below \(24 ext{ ppm}\) (Figure 4).

The bioaerosol clearance curves (Figure 5) demonstrate a profound biosecurity contrast: VEGGIE exports \(100\%\) of airborne fungal spores directly into the crew living modules (\(t_{50} = 13.8 ext{ s}\) on High Fan), maximizing astronaut allergen and pathogen exposure. In contrast, APH recirculates air internally through high-efficiency particulate air (HEPA) filters, achieving fast spore scrubbing (\(t_{50} = 18.4 ext{ s}\)) while maintaining zero cabin burden (Table 4).

Discussion

Our findings demonstrate that spaceflight plant hardware design must transition from empirical terrestrial heuristics to rigorous microgravity fluid dynamics. Specifically, the "stratification paradox" indicates that closed ducted chambers can achieve superior turbulence and boundary-layer stripping in microgravity relative to Earth controls. For future Lunar and Martian surface BLSS facilities, we recommend: (1) adopting dual opposing lower lateral supply diffusers to ensure \( arepsilon_a > 45\%\), (2) decoupling bulk air exchange (\( ext{ACH} \sim 100 - 300 ext{ h}^{-1}\)) from canopy shear velocity (\(U \sim 0.3 - 0.6 ext{ m/s}\)), and (3) integrating localized HEPA filtration to safeguard crew habitat air quality.

Methods

Simulations utilized OpenFOAM v2606 with compressible Navier-Stokes and Low-Mach approximations. Turbulence was modeled using the \(k ext{-}\omega ext{ SST}\) RAS formulation with prism boundary layers (\(y^+ \sim 1 - 5\)). Geometric surfaces were synthesized via analytic Python generators with 0 open/duplicate edges. Three-level mesh convergence ladders (\(m0/m1/m2\)) confirmed grid independence within \(<1.3\%\) error.
